A2 · Pretérito indefinido / pretérito perfecto simple

Tell complete past events clearly

What this tense does

Use the pretérito indefinido to present a past event as complete. It can describe one action, a sequence, a completed stretch of time, or repeated actions inside a closed past period.

Examples of viewpoint
One event: Ayer reservé una habitación.
Sequence: Llegué, saludé y pregunté por mi habitación.
Completed period: Viví allí cinco años.
Repeated in a closed period: Durante esas vacaciones, caminé por la playa todos los días.

Time expressions that often help

ayeranteayeranocheel otro díala semana pasadahace dos díasen 2022de enero a marzo

These expressions help locate an event, but they do not automatically choose the tense. A sentence can combine imperfecto for background and indefinido for the event.

Regional variation matters

With expressions such as hoy or esta mañana, speakers vary by region and perspective. Forms like hoy comí are natural in many varieties, while hoy he comido is frequent in much of Spain. Treat this as variation, not a simple correct/incorrect rule.

Scope of the source lesson

The core practice is regular -ar, -er and -ir conjugation. The source also flags forms that need extra attention later, such as busqué, leyó and durmió. It notes that a present-tense stem-changing verb like contar can still take a regular indefinido ending: contó.

Accents matter
In these regular models, yo and él/ella/usted forms carry written accents where shown.
No extra -s for tú
Use hablaste, comiste, viviste — not forms ending in an extra -s.
Context can disambiguate
Regular -ar and -ir nosotros forms can look identical in present and past: hablamos, vivimos. Context tells you which tense is intended. -er differs: comemos → comimos.

Ustedes uses the same forms as ellos/ellas. Vosotros/vosotras is used mainly in Spain. If you use vos, the source lesson practices the standard forms hablaste, comiste and viviste.

Is pretérito indefinido the same as pretérito perfecto simple?

Yes. In this lesson they are two names for the same tense. Do not confuse it with the compound perfect, such as he hablado.

Do -er and -ir verbs use the same endings?

Yes: -í, -iste, -ió, -imos, -isteis, -ieron.

Why can hablamos be present or past?

The forms coincide for regular -ar nosotros. Context distinguishes them. The same issue occurs with regular -ir forms such as vivimos and escribimos.

Can indefinido describe long or repeated actions?

Yes, if the speaker presents the period as a completed whole. Duration or repetition alone does not force the imperfecto.

What should I study next?

Once these endings feel automatic, continue with irregular pretérito indefinido forms and then practice the contrast between indefinido and imperfecto.

Quick close

Tell three ordinary things you did yesterday. The point is automatic, usable grammar—not dramatic stories.
